# Marketing and Customer Strategy

Customer acquisition cost (CAC) should be evaluated against customer
lifetime value (LTV); a healthy LTV:CAC ratio is generally 3:1 or higher.
Marketing spend concentrated on products or regions already showing positive
revenue trends tends to produce a higher return than spend aimed at
declining segments, because it reinforces existing demand momentum instead
of fighting against it. Retention-focused campaigns (loyalty programs,
win-back offers) are typically cheaper per incremental dollar of profit than
new-customer acquisition, especially in categories with high repeat-purchase
rates. Seasonal or anomaly-driven demand spikes should be investigated
before being used to plan future marketing budgets, since they may not
repeat.
