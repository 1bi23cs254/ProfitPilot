# Pricing Strategy

Products operating at a net margin below 10% are strong candidates for a price
review before any volume-growth investment, since additional volume at a thin
margin compounds risk rather than profit. A 3-7% price increase on
low-elasticity, high-demand products typically has minimal impact on unit
volume while directly improving gross margin. Bundling low-margin products
with high-margin complements can preserve customer-perceived value while
lifting the effective margin of the bundle. Dynamic/tiered pricing by customer
segment or region captures willingness-to-pay differences that flat pricing
leaves on the table. Before raising prices, validate elasticity using
historical volume response to any past price change if that data exists.
